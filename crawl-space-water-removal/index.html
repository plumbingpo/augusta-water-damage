Crawl Space Water Removal: What Augusta Homeowners Should Know

Few homeowners notice right away when water collects beneath the floor. It tends to turn up as a damp chill underfoot, a stale smell at the vents, or a small lake discovered the day somebody lifts the hatch. By then the insulation is usually soaked and the wood above it has been wet for days. Crews from our company extract the water, haul away anything that cannot be saved and dry the structure ahead of rot.

Pumping is just half of the work. Floor joists, beams and the subfloor absorb moisture slowly and let go of it slowly, so we take moisture readings on the wood and keep dehumidifiers running until the numbers come down, usually 3 to 5 days. Finding the cause matters just as much, since a dripping water line, a failed drain or groundwater from a storm will flood the area again unless it is addressed.

What It Looks Like

  • Joists, beams or subfloor panels coated with whitish or blackish fuzz
  • Puddles or wet muck visible just inside the crawl space door
  • Fiberglass insulation sagging or hanging down between the floor joists
  • An odor like damp soil that gets stronger close to the registers
  • Moisture loving pests such as camel crickets and silverfish appearing indoors
  • Dark water marks and chalky deposits partway up the block or concrete

How We Fix It

Our Crawl Space Water Removal Process

1

Inspect and Make Safe

Nobody enters until the space has been screened for shock risks, hanging ducts and contamination. Then we map how far the water reached and take moisture readings on the joists and subfloor.

2

Pump Out Water

Standing water comes out with submersible pumps and hoses, down to the footing trenches and dips where it gathers. Any silt or mud sitting on the liner gets removed during this step.

3

Remove Wet Materials

Soaked insulation, ruined vapor barrier and debris are bagged and carried out. Where mold has begun or is likely, the bare joists and subfloor get cleaned and an antimicrobial applied.

4

Dry and Verify

Low profile air movers and a dehumidifier run under the house until the wood reaches a normal moisture reading. You get the closing moisture readings plus a plan for keeping water out, after we verify everything by meter.

The Damage Timeline

Day one

Puddles Form Underneath

The dirt floor starts absorbing moisture as puddles form in every dip. Batts begin drawing moisture upward and the air below the house turns humid quickly.

Hours 24 to 48

Early Mold Growth

Growth may begin on wet framing and on the paper backing of the batts. The weight of the water drags the batts loose from the underside of the subfloor.

Days 3 through 7

Moisture Moves Upstairs

The moisture starts reaching the part of the house you live in. You might see floorboards cupping and notice a musty odor while the framing continues to soak.

Past one week

Rot Sets In

Lumber left damp this long starts breaking down. Joists and sill plates soften, fasteners rust and the damp space attracts termites and other pests.

Hidden Risks

What Hides Behind It

Shifting Piers and Supports

Long periods of standing water loosen the soil that holds up the support piers. Doors that stick and floors that slope upstairs are possible signs.

Wet Wiring and Ducts

Electrical runs, junction boxes and ductwork pass through most crawl spaces. Submerged wiring is a shock hazard and soaked duct insulation spreads musty air through the system.

Termites and Other Pests

Rodents, carpenter ants and termites all seek out moist lumber and soggy ground. Once the area is dry it becomes far less inviting.

Air From Below

Much of what you breathe on the main level drifts up from beneath the house, carrying any mold spores and musty smells along with it.
